SPN 1056 — Electronic Pressure Control, Trailer Control

Safe to driveDrivable, but watch it yourself

Updated: 2026-11-10

J1939 code SPN 1056. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Bendix ABS/ESP, KAMAZ EBS3, Knorr-Bremse EBS, MAN and Renault Trucks, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

Trailer control in the tractor's electronic brake system (EBS): a proportional valve in the trailer control valve. The EBS unit sets the pressure in the trailer's yellow (control) line through it.

What it should read

Pressure in the red (supply) headusually 7.0…8.1 bar
Pressure to the trailernot more than 8.5 bar

Check order

  1. FMI 3, 4, 5, 6: the connector on the trailer control valve — it is on the frame at the back of the cab, in dirt. Check the coil and the wires to the EBS unit for continuity.
  2. FMI 7, 13: the pressure is not what was commanded. Connect a gauge to the yellow head, press the brake, and compare with what the diagnostics shows.
  3. Trailer control valve: dirt and water inside — from a failed air dryer.
  4. A leak in the yellow line up to the head, or in the head — the valve cannot hold the pressure.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Connector on the trailer control valve.
  2. Dirt and water in the valve.
  3. Leak in the line to the trailer.
  4. The valve itself.

What SPN 1056 refers to

SPN 1056 is the electronic pressure control for the trailer control valve in an electronic braking system. The valve sets the brake pressure sent to the trailer line, and the electronics check its circuit and, on many systems, compare commanded and measured pressure.

  • FMI 2 - erratic signal or the pressure does not match the command (a frequent search).
  • FMI 5 - open circuit: the wire, the plug or the coil.
  • FMI 6 and 4 - short to ground or low voltage.
  • FMI 3 - voltage high or shorted to supply.
  • FMI 7 - the valve is mechanically stuck.

Checks

  • The 7-pin and 15-pin connectors between tractor and trailer, and the coiled air hoses: leakage or a loose connection.
  • The trailer control valve connector: corrosion and water.
  • Air pressure in the supply line, with the gauge, to rule out a leak that looks like a valve fault.
  • Brake-pressure sensor in the trailer line, where fitted.

Compare the commanded and measured pressure in the scan tool while applying the brake. A good valve follows the command within a small margin. Only if wiring and plugs are clean should the valve be replaced.

Can you keep driving?

Brake performance of the combination may be reduced, because the trailer brake pressure is not controlled correctly. Drive carefully with a lighter load and shorter speeds until you have it checked.

Questions and answers

Does SPN 1056 appear without a trailer?

It can, if the system tests the valve circuit regardless. A code with no trailer points to the tractor-side valve or harness.

Could it be only a connector problem?

Yes, quite often. Check connectors and hoses first.

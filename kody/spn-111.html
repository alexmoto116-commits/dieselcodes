SPN 111 — Coolant level

Do not driveUntil the level is checked

Updated: 2026-11-16

J1939 code SPN 111. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Cummins ISB, Mercedes-Benz, Dongfeng and other makers, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The coolant level sensor sits in the coolant reservoir. Usually it is a float with a reed switch, or an electrode sensor on two or three wires. It is often wired not to the engine unit but to the dash.

What it should read

Level in the tank on a cold enginebetween the MIN and MAX marks

Check order

  1. Level in the tank on a cold engine. Low — look for a leak, not a sensor fault: hoses, radiator, water pump, heater, oil cooler.
  2. Coolant disappears with no external trace — it is going into the engine: foam or oil in the tank, white smoke.
  3. Level normal but the code is there — disconnect the connector, inspect it and check the sensor for continuity: the float switches when the tank is tilted.
  4. Float stuck from dirty coolant — flush the tank.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. A real coolant leak.
  2. Float stuck in a dirty tank.
  3. Connector on the tank — the area is wet.
  4. Head gasket or oil cooler — coolant leaks inside.

Can I keep driving

Do not drive. Stop as soon as it’s safe to do so, check the coolant level and find the leak before you go on.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: until the level is checked

Low coolant does not overheat the engine at once, but the risk is real, especially under load or on a long climb. Do not drive until the coolant is topped up and the cause of the loss is found.

If the engine does overheat, an aluminum head can warp and the gasket can burn through, which can end in a damaged head or a cracked block. Shut it down and let it cool — never open the coolant reservoir cap while hot.

If the level in the reservoir is normal and only the sensor circuit is faulty (FMI 3, 4), the engine is not necessarily hot. But the truck then cannot warn you about a real coolant loss, so check the level by hand and watch the temperature gauge (SPN 110).

What to check on the spot

Let the engine cool — never open the coolant reservoir cap while hot. Then check the level and the hoses.

Which codes follow

If the value really did go out of range, rather than a sensor circuit being broken, these normally light up after it:

  • SPN 1108Engine protection timer override
  • SPN 1569Torque limited (engine protection)

They are not fixed separately — they go out together with this one. The list is deliberately short: only the links the reference is sure about.

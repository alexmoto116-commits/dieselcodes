SPN 157 — Injector Metering Rail Pressure #1

Drive with cautionTo a shop, do not put it off

Updated: 2026-11-16

J1939 code SPN 157. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Cummins ISB, DAF, Detroit Diesel, Dongfeng, Foton, Isuzu, KAMAZ, MAZ, Renault Trucks, SITRAK, YaMZ and ZF AS-Tronic, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The fuel pressure sensor sits on the end of the common rail. Three-wire: 5 V reference, ground and signal. The ECU regulates the pressure from it, so this code almost always comes with loss of power or the engine stopping.

What it should read

Reference voltage at the connector5.0 V ± 0.25
Signal with the engine offabout 0.5 V
Pressure for startingusually not less than 200…300 bar — check against the factory data

Check order

  1. FMI 3, 4 (electrical): unplug the connector, inspect the contacts, and with the ignition on measure the 5 V reference. With the engine off the sensor should show close to zero pressure: if it shows hundreds of bar — the sensor is at fault.
  2. FMI 1, 17, 18 (pressure low): fuel first — level, coarse and fine filters, transfer pump. Air in the clear hose or bubbles in the sedimenter — an air leak on the suction side.
  3. Check the leak-off from the injectors: disconnect the returns and compare how much each one returns on cranking or at idle. If one returns noticeably more than the others — that injector is pulling the rail pressure down.
  4. Check the pressure regulating valve on the high-pressure pump and the rail's pressure relief valve (the rail has a drain pipe): a leak from the relief valve with the engine running — it has opened or is faulty.
  5. FMI 0, 16 (pressure high): pressure regulating valve on the high-pressure pump, clogged drain from the rail.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Clogged fuel filters, air leak on the suction side.
  2. Worn injector with excessive leak-off.
  3. Pressure regulating valve on the high-pressure pump.
  4. Rail pressure relief valve.
  5. Connector or the pressure sensor itself.
  6. Worn high-pressure pump.

Can I keep driving

Drive with caution, to a shop. Rail pressure is not holding: power drops and the engine may stall. Red lamp or loss of power: pull over at the first safe spot.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: To a shop, do not put it off

Fuel pressure in the common rail is not holding. Per the Detroit bulletin power drops by about 25 %; some makes limit power and rpm, and with a large pressure loss the engine may stall.

Drive to a shop without hard loads and do not put it off. Red lamp or loss of power: pull over; check the fuel filters and air in the system first, then leaks, the sensor and its connector.

What to check on the spot

Check the fuel filter and water trap, drain the water. In cold weather, suspect fuel waxing.

Which codes follow

If the value really did go out of range, rather than a sensor circuit being broken, these normally light up after it:

  • SPN 652Engine Injector, Cylinder #02
  • SPN 653Engine Injector, Cylinder #03
  • SPN 655Engine Injector, Cylinder #05
  • SPN 1569Torque limited (engine protection)

They are not fixed separately — they go out together with this one. The list is deliberately short: only the links the reference is sure about.

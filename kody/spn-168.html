SPN 168 — Battery voltage

Updated: 2026-11-14

Safe to driveTo the repair shop

J1939 code SPN 168. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Cummins ISB, DAF, Detroit Diesel, Dongfeng, International, KAMAZ, KAMAZ-5490, MAZ, Mercedes-Benz, Renault Trucks, SITRAK and YaMZ, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The ECU measures the voltage at its own power input. This code means the ECU sees the wrong voltage: the cause is in the batteries, the alternator, or the wires and grounds between them and the ECU.

What it should read

Two charged 12 V batteries, engine offvoltage normal for a 24 V on-board system
Engine running, alternator chargingvoltage higher than with the engine off, but normal for a 24 V system
Battery terminals and the ECU input under loadnearly the same voltage, no noticeable drop

Check order

  1. Measure at the battery terminals with the engine off, then with it running.
  2. Voltage above normal with the engine running — alternator regulator. Below normal — alternator, belt, or the wire from the alternator.
  3. Battery voltage is normal but the ECU sees less: measure at the ECU power connector with a load on (headlights, heater fan). A noticeable drop means poor contact: the battery terminals, the ECU ground, the engine-to-frame ground, the power fuse.
  4. The code appears only when cranking — check the batteries with a load tester or meter: a sagging battery drops the voltage and the ECU restarts.
  5. If several ECUs have this code at once, the cause is common: batteries, alternator or the main ground, not the ECU.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Corroded or loosely tightened battery terminals.
  2. Engine-to-frame ground or ECU ground.
  3. Worn or discharged batteries.
  4. Alternator regulator, brushes, belt.
  5. ECU power fuse or relay with burnt contacts.

Can I keep driving

You can drive to the nearest repair shop, but do not put it off — if only the amber lamp is on and power is normal. A red lamp, a drop in power or trouble starting — stop.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: To the repair shop

The supply voltage the ECU sees is out of range — high or low, depending on the FMI. While only the amber lamp is on and power is normal, the engine usually keeps running.

The cause is most often the alternator and its belt, the terminals and grounds, or weak batteries. Do not put it off: if the supply keeps sagging, some functions may be limited.

In J1939 FMI 0 and 1 are the most severe level; for FMI 17 and 18 the sources describe an amber lamp. If in doubt, it is safer to stop and look on the spot.

What to check on the spot

Check the battery terminals and the ground — corrosion and loose terminals scatter false codes across the whole truck. Then measure the voltage with the engine off and running: with the engine running the alternator should be charging, and the voltage should stay within the normal range of the 24 V on-board system.

SPN 3719 — Diesel particulate filter (DPF) 1 soot load, %

Drive with cautionTo the nearest safe stop

Updated: 2026-11-10

J1939 code SPN 3719. No factory wording for it in the reference — below is the standard meaning of FMI.

How to check it yourself

Soot load of diesel particulate filter (DPF) 1, in percent: how much soot the engine control unit estimates has built up in the filter. The code is not a sensor failure but a message that the filter is filling up and the soot has to be burned off. The unit judges the load partly from the pressure drop across the filter (see code 3251).

What it should read

Filter soot load per diagnostics (percent)drops noticeably after a regeneration; the trigger thresholds per the manufacturer's manual
Pressure drop across the filter (code 3251)per the manufacturer's manual

Check order

  1. FMI 15 (data above normal, least severe level): there is a lot of soot but no urgency. Let the truck complete a normal regeneration on the road and do not switch the engine off at its start; if the filter lamp is on, follow the vehicle's manual.
  2. FMI 16 (above normal, moderately severe level; Detroit: "Soot Level High"): do a parked regeneration. Park outside on level ground with the parking brake set, away from anything flammable: the exhaust is very hot during regeneration. Do not interrupt a regeneration once started, and clear the codes afterwards.
  3. FMI 0 (above normal, most severe level; Detroit: "Soot Level Very High"): engine power may be limited. Do a parked regeneration as for FMI 16. If it does not help, go to a workshop: manufacturers prescribe diagnostics, for example the onboard filter cleanliness tests at Navistar.
  4. FMI 31: per Detroit Diesel, an informational "silent" code logged when the regeneration lamp is flashing; a stationary regeneration is required if the code is active, and nothing is needed if it is inactive. For other makes the meaning of FMI 31 on this number is not confirmed.
  5. If the code returns after a regeneration: check the differential pressure sensor and its tubes (code 3251), whether regeneration is permanently inhibited (the inhibit switch, code 3695), and whether the truck only makes short trips.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Long driving without regeneration: short trips, a cold engine.
  2. Regeneration inhibited by the switch or interrupted.
  3. The differential pressure sensor on the filter or its tubes.
  4. A clogged filter: ash does not burn off during regeneration.

Can I keep driving

You can drive on while the DPF lamp is steady and power is not reduced — plan a regeneration. If the lamp flashes or is red, or power has dropped, stop and run a parked regeneration per the manufacturer’s procedure; if that fails, see a shop.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: To the nearest safe stop

Soot builds up in the filter faster than regeneration burns it off. The lamp warns in stages: steady means the filter needs a regeneration, flashing means it is urgent, with check engine the power is already being reduced, red means the truck must not be operated.

While the lamp is steady and power is normal you can reach a suitable place: choose where to regenerate and do not put it off. If it flashes, comes on with check engine or power has dropped, stop and run a parked regeneration per the manufacturer’s procedure without interrupting it; with a red lamp do not drive on, call a shop. Ash does not burn off: a neglected filter is cleaned or replaced at a shop. The fix is regeneration, cleaning or replacement, not bypassing the filter.

What to check on the spot

Check the DPF lamp: steady, flashing, or with check engine. Make sure the regeneration inhibit switch is not on. Common causes are short trips and long idling, which keep the exhaust too cool to clean the filter; less often the differential pressure sensor, its tubes or an injector. Then act by the lamp stage: regeneration on the road or a parked one per the manufacturer’s procedure (do not interrupt it). If the lamp stays on afterwards you need a scanner and a shop.

Which codes follow

If the value really did go out of range, rather than a sensor circuit being broken, these normally light up after it:

  • SPN 131Exhaust back pressure
  • SPN 1569Torque limited (engine protection)

They are not fixed separately — they go out together with this one. The list is deliberately short: only the links the reference is sure about.

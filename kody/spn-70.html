SPN 70 — Parking Brake Switch

Do not driveMinutes

Updated: 2026-11-14

J1939 code SPN 70. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Ford Trucks, International, KAMAZ-5490, Mercedes-Benz, Renault Trucks and ZF AS-Tronic, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The parking brake switch: it tells the unit whether the pneumatic parking brake is applied. Automatic application and protection against moving with the brake on depend on it.

What it should read

Switch state with the brake applied and releasedchanges with the brake — checked per diagnostics
Parking brake circuit pressureper the manufacturer's manual

Check order

  1. Apply and release the parking brake on a stationary vehicle and watch the switch state in diagnostics.
  2. FMI 2, 13, 19 (unreliable signal, vehicle moving): if in doubt, stop at once; check the switch connector and the adjustment of the parking brake mechanism.
  3. FMI 9, 19 (over CAN, arriving with gaps): see the J1939 bus sections higher up the list — the general methods are the same.
  4. FMI 7 (circuit fault): check the parking brake air system for leaks with soapy water.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Stuck switch or its oxidised connector.
  2. Leak in the parking brake air system.
  3. Adjustment of the parking brake mechanism.
  4. CAN bus gaps from the brake unit.

Can I keep driving

Do not drive. Stop as soon as it’s safe to do so — don’t keep going.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: Minutes

Part of the brake circuit or ABS/EBS is out of service. The pedal may still feel normal — until the first emergency stop or descent.

Next comes longer stopping distance, a skid on a slick surface, failure on a long downgrade. This is the case where "I will take it slow" does not work.

What to check on the spot

Let the air system build to working pressure and listen for leaks. Don’t drive with faulty brakes.

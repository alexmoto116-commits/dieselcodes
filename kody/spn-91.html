SPN 91 — Accelerator pedal position

Check before drivingThrottle response may be limited or unpredictable

Updated: 2026-11-16

J1939 code SPN 91. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Cummins ISB, Detroit Diesel, DAF, Mercedes-Benz, Renault Trucks, Isuzu and other makers, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The accelerator pedal (gas pedal) sensor sits right on the pedal. It has two independent channels inside: the second (SPN 29) lets the ECU catch an error in the first. Many pedals also have an idle switch (SPN 558).

What it should read

Reference voltage at the connector, ignition on5.0 V ± 0.25
First channel: pedal released — pressed to the floorusually about 0.5…1 V — 3.5…4.5 V
Second channelon many pedals half of the first — per the factory data

Check order

  1. In diagnostics watch the pedal position in percent: released — 0, floor — 100, and smoothly, without dropouts when pressing slowly.
  2. Dropouts when pressing slowly — the resistive track is worn: the pedal needs replacing.
  3. FMI 3, 4: the connector under the pedal — water from shoes and dirt from the mat get in there. The wire chafes on the floor with every press.
  4. If the code comes together with SPN 29 and 558 — a shared connector or pedal supply.
  5. After replacing the pedal, some makes need the end positions taught.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Worn sensor in the pedal.
  2. Water and dirt in the connector under the pedal.
  3. Wire chafed at floor level.
  4. End positions not taught after replacement.

Can I keep driving

Do not assume it is safe to drive. SPN 91 identifies the accelerator pedal position parameter; the exact fault, severity and correct response depend on the FMI, symptoms and vehicle manufacturer's instructions. If accelerator response is abnormal or unpredictable, stop in a safe place and arrange professional diagnosis or recovery.

What happens if you keep driving

Driving decision: Check FMI and vehicle symptoms first

This is an electrical fault: an open, a short or a bad contact in the pedal sensor circuit. How the engine reacts depends on the make: some trucks drop to idle, some lose power or go into limp-home mode, some keep pulling normally.

Do not rely on a general statement that the truck can be driven to a shop. If accelerator response is abnormal or unpredictable, stop in a safe place and arrange diagnosis or recovery. Consult the manufacturer's instructions for the exact FMI and vehicle. Until the pedal circuit is repaired, throttle response may change without warning.

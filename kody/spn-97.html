SPN 97 — Water in fuel indicator

Detailed breakdown

  • KAMAZ, SPN 97: water in the filter - when to drive, what to drain

What this code means

SPN 97 is the water-in-fuel indicator. A sensor sits in the water separator (the sedimenter of the coarse fuel filter) and tells the engine computer when water collects at the bottom of the bowl. The engine computer then turns on a warning, and on some engines it reduces power.

Water is the enemy of the fuel system. It does not burn, so it causes poor combustion, rust inside the fuel pump and injectors, and it can feed microbial growth in the tank that clogs the filters. This is why the code should be taken seriously even when the engine runs normally.

The second half of the code, the FMI, tells you what the sensor reports. FMI 3 and FMI 4 point to the sensor circuit: voltage above normal or shorted high (3), voltage below normal or shorted low (4). When water is actually detected, the FMI shows how serious it is, and the exact number depends on the make: for example FMI 15 or 16 on many engines, and FMI 18 (critical level) on some DAF engines. What to do on the road

If the light comes on while the engine runs normally, drain the separator at the first safe stop, provided you have a drain valve, a clear container and the right tools. Water settles at the bottom, so drain until clean fuel comes out, not just a few drops.

If the engine loses power, stumbles or starts badly, stop as soon as it is safe and call for help. Note where you last refuelled. If the code comes back after draining, or the water returns within a day or two, do not keep driving on that fuel: go to a service point.

What to check in the workshop

Check the sensor connector and the wiring first, using the wiring diagram for the make. Corrosion at the connector at the bottom of the filter is a common cause. Clean the sensor electrodes; a dirty sensor can give a false reading.

Drain the separator and the tank sediment, then inspect the bowl for cracks. Check the tank breather and the filler cap: a damaged cap or breather lets in water through condensation as the temperature changes. Ask where the fuel was bought, because many water cases come from one bad delivery.

Replace the filter element as the service schedule says, not only when a code appears. In winter the water in the bowl can freeze and block the drain: warm it up, and do not replace the sensor just because the reading is wrong.

Replace the sensor only after the connector, the wiring and the separator are confirmed good, and check the manufacturer manual for any reset or calibration after replacement.

How to check it yourself

The water-in-fuel sensor sits in the sedimenter of the coarse filter (separator). Water is heavier than fuel and collects at the bottom; the sensor detects it by conductivity.

What it should read

Drain the sedimentat every service and immediately on the signal

Check order

  1. Drain the sediment from the filter-separator into a clear container. If there is water, the fault is not the sensor: drain until clean fuel comes out.
  2. Water comes back in a day or two — there is a lot of water in the tank: drain the tank sediment and recall where you refuelled.
  3. No water but the signal is there — disconnect the sensor connector, inspect it and wipe the electrodes; check whether the clear bowl has cracked.
  4. In winter the water in the bowl freezes: warm it up, do not replace the sensor.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Water in the fuel — a real one.
  2. Dirt on the sensor electrodes.
  3. Corroded connector at the bottom of the filter.
  4. Cracked separator bowl.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: To the nearest safe stop

Fuel is not arriving in the right amount or at the right pressure. It shows up first on climbs and under load.

You will most likely stall out on the road, and may not restart on the shoulder. A clogged filter or water in the tank you can nurse to a shop; a failing injection pump you cannot.

This is an electrical fault: an open, a short or a bad contact in the sensor circuit. The value it measures is most likely fine. You can drive. But until the circuit is repaired this system runs blind and cannot warn you about a real failure.

What to check on the spot

Check the fuel filter and water trap, drain the water. In cold weather, suspect fuel waxing.

Questions and answers

Can I keep driving with SPN 97 on?

If the engine runs normally and there is no red lamp or power loss, you can drive to a service point, but drain the separator first if you can. If the engine loses power or stumbles, stop.

The light goes off after draining. Is the fuel fine now?

Not necessarily. Water often comes back from the tank, so check the tank sediment and the breather, and refuel at a trusted station.

Do I need a new sensor?

Usually not first. Check the connector, the wiring and the separator bowl. Replace the sensor only when they are good.

Why does the code appear in winter?

Water in the bowl can freeze and block the drain, and the sensor can read wrongly while it is cold. Warm the bowl and check the drain before changing parts.

Reading SPN 97 by FMI

The FMI is the half of the code that says how the water sensor is failing. These are the values people search for most, based on SAE J1939-73 and the usual Cummins listings.

  • SPN 97 FMI 15 - water detected, data valid but above normal (some engines add FMI 16 as a second, higher stage). On Cummins this is fault code 418. It lights the warning but does not by itself cut power.
  • SPN 97 FMI 3 - circuit voltage above normal, or shorted high. In practice this often means an open circuit: an unplugged connector, a broken wire, or a sensor left disconnected after a filter change (Cummins code 428).
  • SPN 97 FMI 4 - circuit voltage below normal, or shorted low: a wet connector, a chafed wire, a failed sensor (Cummins code 429).
  • SPN 97 FMI 2 - erratic or intermittent signal: a corroded pin or a harness that moves with vibration.

Step-by-step check

1. Drain the separator into a container until clean fuel with no cloudy layer comes out. Water is heavier than diesel and sits at the bottom.

2. Clear the code and run the engine. If FMI 15 returns within a day or two, take a sample from the tank bottom into a clear jar and let it settle: a separate water layer means the tank or the supplier is the problem.

3. For FMI 3, 4 or 2, look at the sensor connector first. Green corrosion, a loose latch or a wire rubbing on the bracket are the usual findings.

4. Only then test or replace the sensor.

Water matters beyond the warning: modern high-pressure common-rail pumps rely on diesel for lubrication and water shortens their life quickly, so a repeat code is worth chasing.

More questions

What is the difference between SPN 97 FMI 3 and FMI 15?

FMI 15 means the engine computer thinks there is water. FMI 3 means the electrical circuit is open or shorted high, and there may be no water at all.

Will the engine derate with SPN 97 FMI 15?

Usually not on its own; it is a warning. But if water reaches the injection pump the engine can lose power, so drain it promptly.

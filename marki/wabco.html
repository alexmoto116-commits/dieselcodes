WABCO ABS/ESC codes: what to know

WABCO ABS/ESC is a table of brake electronics: 28 codes under J1939. Most of the codes concern the brakes. The codes that mean stop are listed first on the page. If the reading has several ABS codes together with CAN codes, check the connector and the unit power first, not the wheel sensors.

How to read a code

A code has two halves. SPN is what is actually at fault — the sensor, component, or parameter. FMI is what is wrong with it — value out of range, open circuit, short circuit, invalid data. So “SPN 100” alone is not a diagnosis yet, but “100/1” already tells you oil pressure has dropped below normal.

If there are several codes at once

That is almost always how it goes: one fault drags five or six codes behind it. An empty AdBlue tank first throws a level error, then interrupted dosing, then a NOx excess and a torque derate. What WABCO codes mean on the truck

WABCO (part of the ZF group since 2020) supplies brake electronics for most European and many other tractors and trailers. ABS and EBS faults are read with a scan tool over CAN. The amber ABS lamp means anti-lock is partly or fully disabled; the trailer reports its own lamp through the ISO 7638 connector.

The air brakes themselves keep working because the pneumatics are independent of the electronics. What you lose is anti-lock protection and related functions such as ASR and ESC.

Typical FMI values in WABCO codes

  • FMI 3 and 4 - circuit voltage high or low: sensor, valve or supply.
  • FMI 5 and 6 - open circuit or short to ground in a modulator valve circuit.
  • FMI 1, 2, 7, 8, 10 - at a wheel speed sensor: weak, uneven or vanishing signal. Look at the gap, the tone ring and the wheel bearing.
  • FMI 9 - lost communication on CAN with another unit.
  • FMI 12 - bad intelligent device, i.e. the unit itself. Conclude this last.

Causes, most frequent first

  • Wheel speed sensor and its cable, especially on the front axle where the cable bends with steering.
  • Tone ring: dirt, damaged teeth or rust.
  • Modulator connectors and harness along the chassis: corrosion, chafing, water.
  • Supply and ground of the ABS unit: a voltage drop produces codes for several axles at once.
  • The ISO 7638 trailer connector: corroded pins create "phantom" wheel faults.
  • The modulator or the unit itself, after everything else.

Short check procedure

1. Read codes and freeze-frame data. 2. Check supply and ground. 3. Inspect connectors, starting with the ISO 7638 plug on the trailer. 4. Measure the wheel sensor: a WABCO sensor is typically around 0.9-1.3 kOhm; compare with the sensor on the same axle. 5. Measure the modulator coil and test the wiring. 6. Replace parts only after that.

Can you keep driving?

Yes, carefully and not for long: the brakes work but without anti-lock. On a slippery road a wheel can lock. Keep extra distance and have the fault repaired.

Questions and answers

The lamp is on but the sensor and valves test fine. What else?

Supply and ground of the ABS unit, the ISO 7638 plug and the CAN network. Check those before the unit.

What is the difference between ABS and EBS?

ABS only prevents wheel lock. EBS also controls braking electronically and exchanges data with the trailer over CAN.

Why do front axle sensors fail more often?

Their cables flex every time the wheels turn, and they are exposed to spray from the tyre.
